# Alumni Portal: sales deck narrative

One generic deck for any JNTUK or JNTUA engineering college. There is no
college-specific version. 16 slides.

**Audience:** principals, trust members, T&P officers and alumni coordinators.
**Maker:** ifBash, Vijayawada. ifBash appears on the cover and the contact
slide only. This deck sells the alumni portal. It is not an ifBash company
deck.

## The storyline in one paragraph

A college loses touch with each batch at convocation. The final-year batch
needs exactly those graduates for mentoring and referrals. So the portal
starts with the final-year batch, and they are already on the network when
they graduate. Every alumnus is checked against the degree register the
college already keeps, and the privacy rules are enforced on the server.
The whole portal wears the college's own name. The deck then shows the
screens and the staff console, explains how a college goes live, and is
straight about what is ready and what comes next. It ends on the price
structure and one next step: see the portal in your college's colours.

## Rules for whoever builds the slides

**Honesty labels.** Put these captions under the screenshots, word for word.
Use caption size, not footnote size.

- **DIET screens:** "Shown in DIET's portal (Ganguru, Vijayawada). The colours
  are placeholders until the college sends its own, and there is no crest or
  photo yet. All people, companies and numbers are demo data."
- **Sri Vydehi screens:** "Sri Vydehi University is a fictional demo college.
  The photos are illustrative stock from Pexels and Unsplash. The people in
  them do not endorse the portal."
- **Any number on a slide from a screenshot** (214, 834, 96, 341, 13 and so
  on) carries "demo data" next to it. The slides themselves claim no counts
  for any real college.

**DIET's role.** DIET is the worked example of a real college before its
brand assets arrive. Its name stays out of headlines and body copy. It
appears only in screenshot captions and on the white-label slide.

**Field.** Aubergine ink #2A1830 on paper #FBF8F4. The dark field #22132A is
used on slides 1, 3, 10, 14 and 16. The cover (1) and closer (16) are
exempt from the one-in-three rule. That leaves three dark slides out of the
14 in between.

**Colour roles.**

- **Rose #9E2A4B:** coloured text on light slides. On the dark field use
  rose-on-dark #E88AA3.
- **Mint #2E6A55:** step numbers. On the dark field use mint-on-dark #7FC4A8.
- **Peach #F2865A:** fills only, with an ink label, and at most one peach
  object per slide. The suggested places are the rollover marker on slide 3
  and the call-to-action on slide 16. No other slide needs peach.
- **Clay:** rules and hand-drawn marks. One use is the circle around
  "cannot" on slide 10, as the showcase does.

**Type.** Headlines are Bricolage Grotesque 700. Text is Hanken Grotesk.
Eyebrows are IBM Plex Mono in uppercase.

**Shapes and surfaces.**

- Square corners.
- Hairlines, not boxes.
- No shadows.
- No gradients, except in the mark.

**Screenshots.** Full colour, with a 1px hairline border and no device
frames. That includes the phone shots.

**Logo.** Use the logo as supplied and never recolour it.

- Light slides: `brand/final/logo-primary-aubergine.png`, or the `.svg` of
  the same name.
- Dark slides: `logo-reversed-paper`.

**Voice.** Plain, specific and unhedged, in sentence case. These words are
banned: leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless,
revolutionary, game-changing.

## Slide 2: The problem

- **Field:** light
- **Eyebrow:** THE PROBLEM
- **Headline:** A college loses touch with each batch at convocation, and
  the usual fixes do not hold it.
- **Body:** four points, in a hairline list
  - **Alumni drift.** After convocation the college keeps a roll number and
    an old email address. Nothing brings the graduate back.
  - **Final-years need referrals.** The people best placed to give them are
    recent graduates the college can no longer reach.
  - **Hosted platforms lapse.** When the subscription ends, the member list
    stays with the vendor unless someone exported it in time.
  - **An open directory is a liability.** A public list of names, batches
    and employers is easy to scrape. Under the DPDP Act, the college answers
    for it.
- **Screenshot:** none

**Speaker notes.** Ask the room three questions before you move on:

1. Does the college use an alumni platform today, or did it use one before?
2. Does anyone hold an export of that member list?
3. Who in the college could find a 2015 graduate's current email this week?

Keep the DPDP point plain and do not give legal advice. The college decides
why member data is collected, so the college is accountable for it. Do not
name competitors on this slide.

## Slide 3: Two cohorts, one network

- **Field:** dark
- **Eyebrow:** TWO COHORTS, ONE NETWORK
- **Headline:** Start with the final-year batch, and your graduates are
  already on the network when they leave.
- **Body:**
  - **One record per person.** A final-year student's account becomes an
    alumni account after convocation. Nobody registers twice.
  - **Once a year, after convocation, one reviewed step moves the batch
    across.** A dry run comes first, and the step never runs on its own.
  - **Final-years give alumni a reason to sign in:** a student from their
    own branch who is asking for help.
  - **Limits protect alumni.** Each student can have five open requests at
    a time and send ten a month.
- **Diagram (instead of a screenshot):** a hairline line with numbered
  stops, numbers in mint-on-dark #7FC4A8:
  01 Final-year roll list → 02 Final-year student → 03 Convocation: yearly
  rollover, with a dry run → 04 Alumni.
  Mark stop 03 with the slide's one peach fill.
- **Screenshot:** none. As an option, use 15 (the student tab bar) as a
  small callout.

**Speaker notes.** Most alumni portals only know about graduates. This one
knows about the students who become them.

The request limits are not decoration. Hundreds of students writing freely
to thousands of alumni is how an alumni network goes quiet. The server
checks the limits on every request, so the app cannot bypass them.

If someone asks how students get their accounts, say it now: accounts come
from the placement cell's final-year roll list, not from an open sign-up
form. The tool that imports that list is on the "Coming next" slide.

## Slide 4: What each group does

- **Field:** light
- **Eyebrow:** WHO IT IS FOR
- **Headline:** Each group on campus gets the part of the network its job
  needs, and no more.
- **Body:** a 2×2 grid separated by hairlines
  - **Alumni:** Find batchmates by batch, branch, company or city. Decide
    who sees your email and phone. Post jobs, mentor, and register for
    reunions.
  - **Final-year students:** Search alumni by branch, batch and company. Ask
    for mentoring or a referral. Apply to jobs that alumni post.
  - **Faculty, HODs and the placement cell:**
    - HODs review registrations from their own department only, and run
      department events.
    - Faculty can search both cohorts.
    - The placement cell approves every job before students see it. CGPA
      stays on the placement side.
  - **Alumni cell:** Works the verification queue, runs events and check-in,
    publishes news and galleries, and writes to batches.
- **Footer line:** Nine roles, one permission table. One person can hold
  several roles and gets exactly what each allows.
- **Screenshot:** none. It is a text slide. As an option, add 16 (faculty
  home) as a thumbnail if the layout has room.

**Speaker notes.** The roles can combine. An alumnus who now teaches and
heads a department holds three roles and gets exactly the union of the
three.

- **Finance** reconciles giving and has no access to the directory at all.
- **The college administrator** holds roles, branding, bulk export and the
  yearly rollover.
- **ifBash's own role** gets its own slide (10).

The point for this room is that nobody gets access just because they are
staff. Each role has only the access its work needs.

## Slide 10: Privacy and DPDP by design

- **Field:** dark
- **Eyebrow:** PRIVACY AND DPDP
- **Headline:** The privacy rules are enforced on the server, and ifBash
  cannot open your members' records.
  - Circle "cannot" in clay, as a hand-drawn mark.
- **Body:** two columns
  - **Between members**
    - Nobody searches your alumni without signing in. There is no public
      list.
    - Contact details need two yeses. The viewer must be entitled, and the
      owner's own setting must allow it.
    - A student sees an alumnus's email or phone only after that alumnus
      accepts the student's request.
    - CGPA and placement status stay with the placement cell. Students
      never see giving.
  - **Between your college and us**
    - Only your college administrator can export contacts in bulk, and the
      export needs a written reason that goes into the audit log.
    - DPDP requests (a copy, a correction or a deletion) start a 30-day
      clock. Only your college administrator can act on them.
    - ifBash's admin account sets up your college, adjusts its branding
      and reads the audit log. It cannot open a profile, search the
      directory, export contacts or see a data request.
    - The portal is designed to keep data in India (AWS Mumbai). Hosting is
      not live yet.
- **Screenshots:** 18-member-profile-desktop.png. **Blur the phone number.**
  It was randomly generated and could belong to a real person. Also use
  39-roles-desktop.png, which shows the locked "Platform provider (ifBash)"
  card.
- **Caption:** the DIET caption. Add: "Phone number blurred."

**Speaker notes.** Hiding a button is presentation. These rules are
permissions, checked on the server on every request. The database schema
separates colleges with row-level security, so isolation does not depend on
application code remembering to filter.

- **18:** The contact card reads "Vandana shares this with DIET alumni and
  faculty". That is the two-yes rule on screen: the viewer is entitled, and
  the owner consented.
- **39:** The ifBash card is locked.

Automated tests hold the line on what ifBash can and cannot see. Say this
plainly: we found a gap here once and closed it, and the tests exist so it
stays closed.

Consent is recorded along with the version of the privacy notice the member
agreed to.

On data residency: the database and file storage are specified for AWS
Mumbai (ap-south-1). The services that deliver email and WhatsApp messages,
and where those services process them, are agreed with each college and
named in its privacy notice.
